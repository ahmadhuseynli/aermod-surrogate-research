# Publication history and research status

This page separates what was published at each stage of the atmospheric dispersion surrogate research from conclusions reached later. Historical scientific writing should be preserved as it appeared at the time. A later result can change the interpretation of an earlier experiment without retroactively changing what the experiment recorded.

The earliest public prototype releases were published on 17 September 2026. Version 0.1.1 is preserved at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22819728, with the parent project DOI at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22819727. The original research note and its statements about the early Phase2H tests belong to that release, not to the final product assessment.

On 18 September 2026, the GitHub public research narrative was expanded and cleaned up. The exact pre-closure publication is recoverable from Git commit ba305acbf9 and is reproduced without textual changes as [the original website](https://ahmadhuseynli.github.io/aermod-surrogate-research/original-publication-2026-09-18.html). An exact copy of the original README and the original supporting scientific chapters is kept under [historical/2026-09-18](historical/2026-09-18/README.md). These historical documents still contain statements about what the research might achieve and how the authors understood the validation at the time. They are not revised into retrospective claims and should not be mistaken for the project's October research status.

On 23 October 2026, a later assessment of the accumulated Gen1, Gen2 and Gen3 evidence concluded that the investigated generic AERMOD surrogate product formulation had reached a practical no-go. This decision is a new research conclusion, not a correction applied to the text of the September publication. Its full scientific discussion is preserved as [the October NO-GO research chapter](RESEARCH_CLOSURE_2026-10-23.md) and as a [separate public reading page](https://ahmadhuseynli.github.io/aermod-surrogate-research/no-go-2026-10-23.html). The initial closure version was archived on Zenodo as 0.2.0, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23604102.

Also on 23 October, the research history was reviewed to identify the closest recorded imitations of AERMOD. That retrospective separates predictions by genuinely trained surrogates from reconstructions that were given AERMOD-derived amplitudes or peaks, and describes each result's mathematics, source, meteorology, receptor field, error metric and evidence role. It is available as [the mathematical closest-match chapter](CLOSEST_AERMOD_MIMIC_CASES_2026-10-23.md) and the [dedicated public chapter page](https://ahmadhuseynli.github.io/aermod-surrogate-research/closest-matches.html). The expanded archival release 0.3.0 is https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23604369.

The later source audit also distinguishes 2018 spent confirmatory evidence from a recovered, spent 2019 year-validation record, which must not be represented as pristine one-shot confirmation. That clarification belongs in the October retrospective and provenance interpretation. The September publication is preserved as written so a reader can see what the public narrative originally said rather than encountering a silently rewritten history.

The practical no-go does not invalidate every restricted-domain result, and the early experiments do not establish a commercially viable generic AERMOD replacement. No released surrogate is claimed to be regulatory-equivalent or approved. All frozen scientific models, teacher results, hashes, prediction seals and governed experimental decisions remain under their original separate scientific authority. The public archive changes described here concern presentation and publication history, not the laboratory's frozen science.
