Independent atmospheric-dispersion research

Can a fast surrogate reproduce AERMOD's hourly concentration field closely enough to be useful inside a validated domain?

This project explores that question from the physics outward. AERMOD is used as the teacher and reference model. The surrogate is trained on hourly dispersion response, tested on meteorological conditions it has not seen during development, and kept within a clearly stated applicability boundary.

The aim is not to replace AERMOD. The useful idea is a two-level workflow: AERMOD remains the trusted simulation and verification engine, while a validated surrogate handles repeated in-domain predictions quickly and sends uncertain cases back to the reference model.

What the first prototype covers

The first problem is deliberately small: one fixed elevated point source, SO2, flat terrain, no building downwash, no chemistry or deposition, and an hourly concentration field as the target.

I kept the first case narrow because that makes failure easier to understand. A poor result can be traced to meteorology, target definition, spatial sampling, receptor geometry or the learner itself instead of being mixed with every AERMOD feature at once.

That mattered in practice. One of the largest early errors came from a coarse teacher grid that could miss narrow plume cores. Increasing model capacity could not recover information that was never represented properly in the first place.

AERMOD remains the reference. The surrogate is a research prototype and is not an approved regulatory substitute.

Why this could be useful

The value of a surrogate is not one faster calculation. It is the ability to explore many physically related cases quickly once the domain has been validated.

Large scenario screening

Run many source, meteorology and receptor combinations quickly before selecting cases that need full reference-model verification.

Sensitivity studies

Explore how the hourly field changes across controlled physical inputs without repeatedly rebuilding a full modelling campaign from scratch.

Adaptive simulation

Use the surrogate to find weak or poorly supported parts of the state space, then generate targeted AERMOD cases there and retrain.

Applicability checks

Attach confidence or support information to each prediction so that unsupported cases are verified instead of guessed.

If the next scientific stages hold up, the same approach could be extended to variable stack conditions, different meteorological regimes, terrain, downwash and multiple sources. Those are future validation problems, not capabilities claimed by the present prototype.

The approximately 87% figure is simply 1 minus the frozen normalised-L1 error. It is a readable summary of this internal whole-field metric, not a universal accuracy score and not a comparison with measured ambient concentrations.

The two years were not copies of the same weather sequence. Same-calendar wind-speed correlation was 0.043, the median absolute wind-direction difference was 75 degrees, and no comparable calendar hour had an identical full dynamic state. I therefore describe the result as same-domain temporal generalisation, not cross-climate transfer.

How the model developed

The important steps were driven by failed tests and changing error mechanisms, not by a calendar or by repeatedly swapping algorithms until a score looked good.

Capacity was not enough

Longer boosted-tree training improved the first model but reached diminishing returns. The remaining error was structured.

Teacher geometry became the issue

Narrow stable plumes exposed limits in the angular receptor grid. The project moved toward plume-relative Cartesian geometry and targeted reconstruction tests.

Fresh-year tests changed the diagnosis

One independent check exposed crosswind weakness; another showed that the dominant error had moved toward longitudinal amplitude and dilution.

Simple residual design survived

Several more complicated successor ideas failed their frozen gates. The model that remained used a strongly bounded local residual correction.

The public repository keeps these failures because they explain the architecture better than a success-only story would.

What held up

The frozen surrogate produced similar whole-field performance in two untouched year-level tests and improved the previous baseline in both.

The result survived materially different hourly meteorology inside the same broad climatology.

What did not

The extreme tail is still weak. In both final year tests, the global maximum was too high and occurred at a different case/receptor from the AERMOD maximum.

That is why I do not claim reliable controlling-receptor or regulatory-peak prediction.

Where the research goes next

The next useful work is not another small tuning cycle on the same setup. It is to widen one physical boundary at a time and see whether the same discipline still holds.

Extreme tail

Improve peak behaviour without fitting directly to already-known validation maxima.

Different climate or site

Test whether the learned response transfers beyond the current broad climatological domain.

Variable source physics

Introduce controlled ranges of stack height, diameter, exit velocity and temperature before moving to more complex source classes.
