Independent atmospheric-dispersion research

A small, controlled test of whether AERMOD's hourly field can be learned by a separate surrogate.

The work started in a private research workspace on 14 August 2026. This site was opened later, after the first development cycle and two one-shot year tests had been frozen. It is a public archive of that work, not the start of it.

What the experiment covers

The first prototype is deliberately narrow: one fixed elevated point source, SO2, flat terrain, no building downwash, no chemistry or deposition, and an hourly concentration field as the target.

I kept the first case small because I wanted to know where the errors were actually coming from. That turned out to matter: one of the biggest early failures was a coarse teacher grid, not simply an underpowered learner.

AERMOD remains the reference. The surrogate is a research prototype and is not an approved regulatory substitute.

The two years were not copies of the same weather sequence. Same-calendar wind-speed correlation was 0.043, the median absolute wind-direction difference was 75 degrees, and no comparable calendar hour had an identical full dynamic state. I therefore describe the result as same-domain temporal generalisation, not cross-climate transfer.

What held up

The frozen surrogate improved the field as a whole in both untouched years, and the longitudinal amplitude correction behaved consistently with the error mechanism identified during development.

The result also survived materially different hourly meteorology inside the same broad climatology.

What did not

The extreme tail is still weak. In both final year tests, the global maximum was too high and occurred at a different case/receptor from the AERMOD maximum.

That is why I do not claim reliable controlling-receptor or regulatory-peak prediction.

The failures shaped the model

The main design changes came after specific tests failed, not from swapping algorithms until a score looked good.

Coarse angular grid

A 10-degree teacher grid missed narrow stable plume cores. Increasing model capacity could not recover spatial information that had never been sampled properly.

Fresh 2024 check

Another crosswind-resolution problem appeared. The geometry was refined, and 2024 was then treated as spent development evidence.

Fresh 2025 check

The main remaining error had shifted toward longitudinal amplitude and dilution, which led to the residual-amplitude layer.
