Can a fast surrogate learn an AERMOD concentration field without pretending to replace AERMOD?

This project uses AERMOD as a teacher and asks a narrower question: inside a clearly defined domain, can a separate model reproduce the hourly spatial response well enough to be useful for fast prediction and research?

The project in one minute

The first prototype is deliberately small: one elevated point source, SO2, flat terrain, no downwash, no chemistry or deposition, and an hourly concentration field as the target. That makes it possible to study the surrogate problem without mixing in every AERMOD capability at once.

The years were not copies of the same hourly weather. Same-calendar wind-speed correlation was 0.043 and the median absolute wind-direction difference was 75 degrees. The claim is therefore same-domain temporal generalisation, not transfer to a different climate or site.

What is working

The surrogate repeatedly improves the full concentration field and the longitudinal amplitude response on unseen years within the restricted domain.

Plume-relative geometry, denser spatial sampling and strict separation between development years and fresh validation years have all been important.

What is not solved

Extreme peak magnitude and peak location are still unreliable. The model has not been validated for other sites, source families, complex terrain, building downwash, multiple sources or chemistry.

Those limits are part of the result, not footnotes.

Why the failures matter

The project changed direction several times because the evidence said it should.

Coarse grid

An early 10-degree angular teacher grid missed narrow stable plumes. More ML capacity could not recover spatial information that was never sampled properly.

Fresh-year failure

A 2024 test showed that a development crosswind spacing was still too coarse. That year was then treated as spent evidence rather than reused as a fresh confirmation.

Amplitude error

A 2025 test showed that the dominant remaining problem was longitudinal amplitude and dilution, which led to the current residual-correction design.
