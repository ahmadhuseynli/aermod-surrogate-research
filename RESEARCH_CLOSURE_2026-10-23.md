# Closing the atmospheric dispersion surrogate research programme

23 October 2026

I began this work with a practical engineering question. AERMOD can be slow to use when the same modelling problem has to be explored across many source settings, weather conditions and receptor locations. If a separate model could learn the hourly AERMOD concentration response, perhaps it could return a credible concentration field in a fraction of the time. AERMOD would still be the reference for verification and regulatory work. The proposed value was the surrogate calculation itself, not a new reporting interface around an existing dispersion model.

After the first prototype and several subsequent generations of experiments, I am closing development of the generic surrogate as a product objective. The evidence does not support promoting the present approach into a reliable, generally usable replacement for repeated AERMOD calculations. Some restricted experiments worked and some individual predictions were good. Those findings are real and remain part of the record. They did not survive the successive questions that matter for a working product: whether the response can be learned without hidden access to the answer, whether it transfers to different weather and source configurations, whether the controlling concentrations can be trusted, whether the applicability rules leave enough useful work for the surrogate, and whether a complete task is demonstrably faster than a properly run AERMOD workflow.

This is a practical no-go decision for the formulation, evidence and product objective investigated here. It is not a proof that no atmospheric-dispersion surrogate could ever work. A different data-generating programme or physical formulation would be a different research proposition, not a continuation that can inherit the validation claims of this one.

## What the early results actually showed

The starting point was intentionally simple: a single elevated point source, sulfur dioxide treated as a passive pollutant, flat terrain, no downwash, and hourly concentration at a controlled set of receptors. The learning target was the concentration field rather than only an annual maximum. Where proportionality to emissions was valid, concentrations were expressed as C/Q so that the learner would concentrate on dispersion rather than on a trivial emissions multiplier.

The first boosted-tree models learned recognizable plume behaviour. Increasing training capacity brought improvement but did not remove structured error. Rotation and mirror tests exposed unnecessary dependence on compass direction, so the feature representation was moved toward plume-relative downwind and crosswind coordinates. That was a scientific improvement, not just a change in algorithm.

A more consequential problem lay in the teacher sampling. On an angular receptor grid, a narrow stable plume could pass between sampled directions. A learner cannot reconstruct a peak that is absent from its training representation. Crosswind reconstruction improved after changing geometry and sampling, while longitudinal amplitude and dilution remained difficult. Fresh meteorological-year diagnostics subsequently shifted the dominant error diagnosis from lateral resolution toward amplitude. Several plausible residual designs were rejected before a tightly bounded residual correction was frozen as Phase2H.

The frozen Phase2H model was tested on 2019 and 2018 without being retrained after the respective truth openings. The normalized whole-field L1 errors were 0.1312534 and 0.1272474. They represented reductions of 23.77% and 24.72% against the earlier frozen surrogate. The often-quoted roughly 87% whole-field agreement was simply one minus this particular normalized L1 statistic. It was not an independently defined accuracy percentage, not a measurement against ambient monitoring data, and not evidence of safe peak prediction. In both years, the global maximum and its location were substantially wrong. In 2018 the reference maximum was about 3.02 on the project's C/Q scale while the surrogate maximum was about 7.11; the corresponding 2019 maxima were about 3.10 and 6.61.

These results were worth publishing because they showed genuine same-domain temporal transfer and helped isolate the remaining failure. They never established a general-purpose dispersion engine. In hindsight, the distinction between improving an average field metric and safely reproducing the controlling part of the field was the first important warning.

## What the wider research programme added

The subsequent Gen2 work developed source-physics experiments, profile-information checks, meteorological diversity, source-by-meteorology-by-receptor interaction tests, holdout designs, applicability and uncertainty rules, and controlled treatment of additional physical complexity. Its Stage24C closure preserved a mature but bounded research capability. This is not being rewritten as failure or deleted because the later product objective failed. What it did not establish was a complete, practically usable surrogate with the coverage and independent end-to-end performance required for the intended engineering product. The frozen Gen1 Phase2H and later governed Gen2 artefacts remain unchanged.

The Gen3 work tried to close that gap under stricter product tests. The regulatory-style point-source programme was particularly instructive. Its original five-year E04 exercise was paused with partial student prediction seals and was not converted into a successful completed comparison. The one-year E04S feasibility work examined whether frozen applicability rules would let the surrogate take over useful work. For four examined source cases, out-of-domain fractions over 8,662 valid 2021 meteorological hours were 46.89%, 10.70%, 43.49% and 24.30%. A subsequent truth-free attribution check traced the sampled exclusions to the existing reference-risk applicability class. Those numbers are evidence of insufficient routing coverage, not evidence that the concentration predictions for excluded cases were wrong. Opening additional teacher truth would not have fixed the routing problem, so it was not done.

The day-level E04A design exposed the same commercial weakness from another direction. With 1,460 scenario-days under examination, the three predeclared routing scopes accepted respectively zero, two and 132 days for surrogate handling. Even the least restrictive scope would leave the planned comparison heavily dominated by AERMOD fallback. That branch was closed before the reserved AERMOD concentration truth was opened. It was a negative feasibility result, not a completed accuracy assessment. A system that sends almost every useful case back to its teacher may preserve scientific caution, but it does not yet deliver the acceleration for which it was proposed.

This distinction is essential. Some branches failed because predictions were inaccurate, some failed because the applicability domain was too narrow, and some ended before teacher truth was opened because the test itself would not answer a useful question. Counting all of them as equivalent failed validation runs would misstate the evidence.

## The final predictive challenges

A later sequence of governed experiments, V8 through V16, asked whether a useful hourly concentration field could be recovered from the available source, meteorological and receptor information. These experiments were not a single interchangeable benchmark. Some examined varied-source data and others returned to one fixed stack; some were representation or truth-assisted ceilings, others trained genuine predictive models. Results from different populations and metrics should not be combined into an artificial learning curve.

The factorization work gave a particularly clear lesson. An oracle supplied with concentration-derived quantities could often describe the field much better than a prediction model that had to infer those quantities from deployable inputs. In V9, an amplitude oracle accounted for a large part of the earlier field error, yet lateral shape and clipping still failed. In V10, adding width and meander structure offered an expressive representation but the blocked predictive learner failed the required convective, low-wind and amplitude-tail checks. V11 obtained a superficially low field error by flattening crosswind profiles and lost important peak behaviour. V12 demonstrated another strong truth-assisted reconstruction ceiling without demonstrating that its amplitude and peak functions could be predicted. This mattered because reconstruction with information extracted from AERMOD truth is not a deployable surrogate.

V13 used a larger already-spent 2019 development population to learn explicit amplitude and peak-related factors. Transfer improved in some respects but still missed convective, low-wind and upper-amplitude gates. V14 introduced physics-related groups within the same learner family and also failed its frozen criteria. V15 tested simpler parametric amplitude representations and did not find a passing low-complexity family. Its screening diagnostic also showed how expensive broad coverage could become: recalling all gridwide top-ten events required retaining all 365 days at the examined setting. That is a finding about the tested plume-following screening task, not a fixed-geographic regulatory-year guarantee.

V16 was designed as a terminal, materially different check rather than another small residual adjustment. A direct neural-field model was trained on the previously spent 2019 population and evaluated on a blocked 2019 holdout and on 64 previously spent selected 2025 transfer cases. The 2019 holdout passed all ten frozen gate families. On the 2025 transfer population, only two of ten passed. Relative whole-field L1 rose to 0.525702. Low-wind relative field L1 was 3.228091 across eight selected cases, the severe-error fraction was 70.31%, median peak error was about 0.4137, and aggregate signed bias was plus 50.65%. These figures concern a selected, already-spent transfer set. They must not be presented as annual population error rates or fresh independent confirmation.

The contrast between 2019 and 2025 was too large to describe as a minor parameter-tuning problem. More importantly, the frozen terminal rule was applied: the current generic full-replacement formulation was closed, and another architecture search under the same premise was not authorized.

## What the October audits clarified

The October 22–23 review did not simply assume that every aspect of the V16 failure was intrinsic to the atmosphere or to neural networks. It checked the case and receptor identities, available manifests, recorded model artefacts, execution provenance and features actually supplied to the learner. Some historical journal and independent-review provenance remained incomplete; those limitations are recorded rather than silently repaired into a stronger scientific claim. The evidence registry was reconciled conservatively, with previously used data kept spent.

One concrete defect was found in the V16 input treatment. Its 6,358 training hours used a wind-reference height of 10 metres, whereas the 64 transfer hours used 7.9 metres. That feature was constant in training. A first-layer neural weight column multiplying the standardized height input therefore remained exactly at its random initialization, and transfer activated a direction the model had never learned. This was a computational mechanism with a testable effect, not a valid reason to relabel a measured 7.9-metre height as 10 metres.

A single reviewed diagnostic disabled that unlearned column while retaining the existing model and previously spent evaluation cases. Overall relative field L1 fell from 0.525702 to 0.243881, low-wind L1 from 3.228091 to 2.396262, and signed aggregate bias from plus 50.65% to plus 19.09%. The severe-error fraction fell from 70.31% to 17.19%. Those are meaningful improvements in the tested computation. Even so, only six of ten historical gate families passed. Overall error, convective error, low-wind error and aggregate bias remained outside the required bounds. The diagnostic was not a new validated model and did not prove that reference-height differences were physically irrelevant. It also did not explain the earlier tree-based failures.

The final coverage audit found another hard limit. The unchanged V16 feature builder did not expose variable source settings to its learner; stack height was fixed at 50 metres, diameter at 2 metres, exit speed at 15 metres per second and exit temperature at 400 kelvin. Additional examples alone could not turn that implementation into the generic variable-source mapping originally sought. Historical meteorological files did contain native 10-metre and 7.9-metre levels, but those levels were entangled with year and weather population. The older 2023 fixed-stack teacher used 756 receptors, compared with the 19,497 receptors in the later V16 geometry. Neither a cross-year height contrast nor a spatial interpolation can be quietly treated as independent physical confirmation.

The audit also found no certified untouched confirmation population with a defensible episode-level independence and precision contract for the proposed narrow fixed-stack rescue. A sensitivity exercise on 2025 meteorological exclusion windows left 6,676 valid hours at plus or minus one day, 4,044 at plus or minus three days, and only 257 at plus or minus seven days. Those were exploratory exclusions, not proof of statistical independence and not thresholds one could choose retrospectively to make a trial succeed. The cumulative available resource budget also remained unreconciled. Accordingly no new experiment was authorized. The October 23 decision retained the generic V16 no-go and classified a possible fixed-stack successor as scientifically conceivable but unqualified.

## What has been learned

The most useful scientific discovery may be that learning the concentration field is not just a matter of giving a flexible algorithm a large table of receptor rows. Receptors from the same hour share the same atmospheric realization. Narrow stable plumes impose a hard sampling requirement, and attractive average-field scores can coexist with unacceptable peak errors. A model may reconstruct an excellent field when given truth-derived amplitude and width, yet fail to predict those quantities from the input information available at inference. A held-out slice from one year can pass while a different year's physical-input support collapses. A confidence or applicability system can be conservatively correct and still leave too little computational work to justify its existence. Even the most impressive restricted-domain accuracy result does not establish end-to-end advantage if the fallback rate is high or full equivalent AERMOD execution has not been benchmarked.

The work also made the evidence rules more precise. Prediction packages have to be sealed before reference truth is opened. Once a set has influenced architecture or thresholds, it becomes development evidence. Technical repairs can restore a run or publication chain, but they cannot retrospectively convert spent cases into independent confirmation. A field metric, a severe-case score, a routing-coverage test, and an engineering decision statistic answer different questions. They must be kept separate even when that makes the public conclusion less flattering.

## Why I am stopping

The original purpose was not to publish the largest collection of machine-learning trials. It was to build a useful independent computation engine that could reproduce AERMOD closely enough over practical source, atmospheric and receptor variation to save real engineering time. That complete result has not been demonstrated. The strongest narrow prototype retained serious peak errors. The later predictive approaches failed difficult transfer conditions. The applicability tests showed that conservative fallback could absorb nearly all the work. The final direct-field challenge failed most of its cross-year gates. A real computational defect explained part, but not all, of its failure. The remaining fixed-stack rescue lacks qualified independent evidence, a complete applicability contract and a reconciled finite resource allocation.

At this point continuing to cycle through models would no longer be a disciplined way to answer the original product question. I am therefore stopping further product development under the present formulation, preserving the closed experiment record and declining to claim that the surrogate is deployable, commercially demonstrated, or equivalent to regulatory AERMOD.

A different future project might begin with a different physical premise, more genuinely informative inputs, prospectively crossed source and weather populations, fresh independent confirmation, and a benchmark on complete engineering tasks. It would need to earn its own evidence. Nothing in this closure authorizes a V17 continuation, reopens frozen Gen1 or Gen2 assets, or permits reuse of spent AERMOD truth as new confirmation.

The final closure is archived as [Zenodo version 0.2.0](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23604102), with the earlier [version 0.1.1](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22819728) preserved. The earlier public Phase2H note and Zenodo version remain part of the historical archive. Their results have not been withdrawn or re-scored. This update changes the interpretation of the programme as a whole: the research yielded useful mechanisms and bounded demonstrations, but it did not produce the generic AERMOD surrogate product for which it was undertaken.
